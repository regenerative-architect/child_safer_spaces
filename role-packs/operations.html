Final master prompt

Develop Child-Safer Spaces, a performance-based, material-neutral program to reduce slips, trips, falls and fixture-impact injuries in businesses children frequent, with access and safety for all customers and workers. Use the U.S. and Canada as initial frameworks, leaving explicit site-specific jurisdiction gates. Treat Walmart only as an example; imply no partnership.

Complete the work sequentially: define scope and stakeholder hypotheses; critically assess evidence and its transferability to children in retail; map laws, authorities and candidate standards; compare interventions, including the controls that could create new hazards; specify procurement and testing of the actual installed system; design a bounded pilot whose conclusions match its power; build an economic model with uncertainty, adverse outcomes and lifecycle costs; assign governance, decision rights and maintenance; prepare adoption materials; and deliver an operational offline HTML bundle.

Separate verified facts from recommendations, illustrative figures and unresolved decisions. Cite primary sources with jurisdiction, scope, edition, retrieval date and limits, and never imply foam, playground testing, or an absence of incidents proves retail safety. Do not invent approvals, efficacy, insurance savings, partners, signatures or funding.

Completion boundary

This bundle fulfils the planning and template deliverables. Actual sites, products, quotes, legal applicability, permits, signatures, customer research and pilot outcomes are not supplied and cannot truthfully be completed here. The workflow assigns each to an owner rather than marking it complete. The list of decisions that still need local professionals, authorities, evidence or signed commitments is in the "Local decisions" section of the Start view and in LOCAL-DECISIONS.md.

Document 02, Business

Program decision and executive brief

Recommendation

Program recommendation: launch a voluntary, evaluated Child-Safer Spaces program. Start with documented hazards and operational controls; consider purpose-designed protective products only where a specific impact hazard survives simpler remedies. Do not install household foam tiles, pool noodles or improvised padding in retail aisles.

Purpose and scope

Reduce avoidable slips, trips, falls and fixture collisions in entrances, aisles, queues and waiting spaces, while keeping access and safety for every customer and worker. Planning population: visitors under 18, reported in the age bands of document 16, with all-visitor and worker balancing measures. Confirm age bands and legal definitions locally.

Decisions requested of the sponsor

  • Appoint a site lead, facilities owner and an evaluation lead independent of product suppliers.
  • Authorize an assessment budget and a capped pilot budget with a separate removal reserve (amounts and currency set by the sponsor; none is assumed here).
  • Adopt the gate sequence and the decision-rights matrix (document 49), including every worker's authority to isolate a hazard.
  • Permit publication of aggregate positive, neutral and adverse findings after privacy review.

Intervention order

First remove the hazard: repair leaks, broken transitions and obstructing fixtures. Next redesign exposure: rounded or recessed fixtures, layout and secure entrance systems. Then strengthen inspection, cleaning and response. Only then evaluate protective products where the remaining mechanism and operating conditions justify it.

What success means

A site can demonstrate that its chosen controls are installed as specified, maintained, accessible and monitored. A pilot can establish operational feasibility long before it can establish injury effectiveness: document 35 shows why a single-site 90-day pilot cannot support an injury-reduction claim. Neither participation nor this workspace creates a safety certification.

Site survey

Map each hazard by zone, mechanism, exposure, existing control, suspected injury route, photographs without people, and immediate action. Survey fixture edges from the floor up to at least the tallest planned child height: for children aged two to five, WHO growth standards put the 97th-percentile height at about 92–93 cm at age two and about 118–119 cm at age five [S37], so a 0–120 cm band is a reasonable program default for that age group. Raise the band for older children. Observe restocking and cleaning as well as trading, and review small-wheel and cane-tip interactions.

Risk triage — program method

Use severity 1–5 and likelihood 1–5 only to sort work. Describe each rating and its uncertainty; multiplying them is not a legal safety determination. Potentially serious hazards, blocked escape or accessible routes, loose parts and unstable walking surfaces are isolated immediately regardless of score. Other work gets an owner and due date; overdue actions escalate to the site manager.

Evidence gaps to commission

Child-retail incidence by mechanism with store denominators; effectiveness of edge guards and fixture redesign; loaded-cart and mobility performance of cushioned systems; aged, wet and cleaned performance; guard durability; life-cycle costs; and comparative injury outcomes. Assign these questions (document 32) before claiming effectiveness.

Adoption sequence

Recruit one sponsor and a small set of suitable sites; engage disability representatives and staff before selecting products. Agree evidence questions with the insurer and authorities, then obtain competing supplier responses. Publish a factual notice before any physical pilot. Review operational findings at 30, 60 and 90 days. Expand only after Gate G6; replication should include different exposure levels and store sizes.

Selection rubric

Score each site 0–2 for documented hazard burden, data quality, management capacity, representative traffic, feasible comparison, and ability to isolate and reverse changes. These are program screening scores, not risk certifications. Exclude sites that cannot keep a safe accessible route or fund removal. Include smaller businesses when maintenance capacity can be supported.

Prevent adoption friction

Offer standard documents, shared testing, training time and procurement options. Keep public claims specific. Do not promise lower premiums, tax credits, grants or a universal return on investment. A large retailer is an example, not an assumed partner.

Accessibility detail (U.S. example)

Source fact: the 2010 ADA Standards require accessible floor surfaces to be stable, firm and slip resistant; carpet must be securely attached with a firm cushion, pad or backing or none, pile no more than 1/2 in, and exposed edges fastened with trim; vertical changes up to 1/4 in are allowed, up to 1/2 in if bevelled no steeper than 1:2, and larger changes must be ramped [S28]. Agreed commitments

The operator maintains ordinary safety controls at every participating site, supplies staff time and logs, and promptly addresses reported hazards. The facilities lead owns design and product configuration. The evaluation lead predefines measures and independently reviews results. The supplier supports traceability, failure investigation and warranty obligations. Disability and community advisors review practical access and communication; participation does not make them guarantors of safety.

Governance

Weekly site review examines open defects, exposure completeness, incidents (by protected reference only), mobility concerns and cost. Monthly steering review includes sponsor, operations, facilities, accessibility, evaluation and risk leads. Declare supplier funding and conflicts; people with a commercial interest do not approve their own product evidence.

Adopt the decision-rights matrix in document 49 as part of this charter. Document 11, Operations

Installation and commissioning checklist

Workflow: W10 in document 49. Needs G0–G3 complete and signed releases.

Before work

Confirm G0–G3 evidence, permits, landlord consent, accepted drawings and the tested assembly. Record installer competence, work schedule and safe public separation. Confirm storage, ventilation/cure instructions and removal materials. Inspect substrate moisture, flatness, damage and contamination against supplier criteria, and record failures before covering them. Post any required notice of temporary disruption to accessible routes or facilities (in Ontario, see [S43]).

During installation

Capture batch/lot numbers, surface preparation, adhesive quantities, cure conditions, seam and trim configuration, and every deviation. Keep access and escape routes open or use an approved alternative. Protect adjacent fixtures and food-related operations. Do not accept unreviewed substitutions to solve a schedule problem.

Before public reopening

  • Verify surface bond, seams, movement, transitions, openings and clearances against approved drawings, including route width and protrusions at every guarded fixture.
  • Repeat the approved slip-resistance check in situ, on the installed and cleaned surface, with the approved method; record instrument validation and conditions.
  • Verify the approved cleaning method, dry condition, cure time and any required tests.
  • Demonstrate mobility aids, strollers, loaded carts and cleaning equipment in a controlled setting with consenting adults, including disabled advisors using their own devices where they agree; no deliberate falls and no child impact trials.
  • Check guard retention, projections, snagging, tear/detachment and cleaning access.
  • Close all critical defects; record non-critical punch-list owners and deadlines.
  • Obtain required authority inspections; hand over as-built details, reports, warranty, spare parts, training and the inspection schedule.

Document 12, Operations

Daily inspection, cleaning and spill response SOP

Workflow: W04 in document 49. Can begin now with existing procedures.

Owner and coverage

The site manager owns this procedure; each shift names a responder and a deputy. Set inspection frequency by hazard, weather, traffic and incident history. Proposed planning points: opening, shift handover, closing, and after cleaning, spills or restocking; high-risk zones need more frequent checks. Record the approved interval in the inspection register. Source fact: U.S. employers must inspect walking-working surfaces regularly and as necessary and correct or guard hazards before employees use them again [S3].

Inspection

Walk entrances, transitions, queues, aisles, cart corrals and guards. Check water, debris, curling, movement, seam gaps, loose parts, contamination, new projections and blocked routes. Record time, zone, defect, action and reopening verification. A blank log is not a completed inspection.

Spill and hazard response

  1. Protect people: physically prevent entry to the affected area and keep or create a safe accessible alternative; notify the shift lead. Source fact: signs and cones only warn — they do not stop people entering; use barriers, close the area or clean in sections [S2]. Never block an emergency exit.
  2. Offer assistance: if an aisle or route is closed, staff retrieve goods or guide customers who cannot use the alternative. U.S. guidance expects merchandise retrieval when an aisle is blocked and temporary interruptions to be remedied as soon as possible [S14][S29].
  3. Identify the hazard and call the appropriate trained responder. Unknown chemicals, blood or other specialist hazards use the existing specialist procedure.
  4. Remove contamination and fix the source with the approved product, dilution and equipment. Keep the area isolated while wet or unsafe; damp mopped floors can be extremely slippery [S2].
  5. Verify dryness, residue, seams, attachment and the surrounding area; record verification before reopening.
  6. Log detection, isolation, clean-up and reopening times in the spill register; investigate repeated sources and escalate repairs.

Immediately isolate lifting edges, unstable surfaces, detached guards, blocked access or sanitation failures. Escalate missed inspections, overdue repair and recurrent wetting to the site manager the same shift. Document interim controls and restart under document 14. Document 13, Operations

Staff training and competency record

Workflow: W10 (before go-live) and W04 (daily) in document 49.

Suggested 25-minute shift module

Five minutes: prevention versus cushioning and the actual site hazards, including carts. Five minutes: demonstrate inspection of entrances, seams, guards and routes. Ten minutes: practise a spill response and an isolation/reopening decision, including how to assist a customer when an aisle is closed. Five minutes: report a concern and complete the log. Adapt language, format and duration to workers' needs; include contractors and temporary staff.

Trainer script

Our purpose is to reduce avoidable hazards for children and everyone else. A soft surface can still slip, move or obstruct wheels. Report defects promptly. Do not improvise repairs or attach household foam to fixtures. Keep the designated safe route open. Signs warn; the site procedure determines how to keep people out of an unsafe area. Only the authorized role reopens the area after checks. Never write a child's name, injury details or contact details into a planning log.

Competency demonstration

Each learner identifies three hazards, isolates a simulated spill without blocking escape, offers assistance to a customer who cannot use the alternative route, locates the approved cleaning instructions, records all four response times, and names who can authorize restart. Repeat instruction if any critical step is missed. Never practise on a live dangerous spill.

Refresher triggers

Document 14, Operations

Incident, stop, removal and restart protocol

Workflows: W11 (stop, suspend, withdraw) and W12 (correct and restart) in document 49.

Incident response

Use the site's emergency procedure and call emergency services when needed. Trained first aiders act within their training; staff do not diagnose injuries. Secure the area and preserve relevant evidence through authorized processes. Notify the manager and the claims contact by the deadlines in the actual policy; record those deadlines in the contact sheet.

Immediate stop triggers — program rules

Any new unstable or lifting surface; a loose or detached guard or small part; a blocked or materially impaired accessible or escape route; a sanitation failure; an installation-related fire concern; or a serious incident plausibly related to the intervention triggers isolation and suspension of the affected installation pending review. Program rule: protective action never waits for proof of causation.

Document 31, Supplier

Fire, hygiene and materials coordination brief

One assembly, several reviewers

The same change can affect flame behaviour, escape, cleaning, moisture and access. Facilities coordinates; fire/code, hygiene, accessibility and operations reviewers each decide within their remit. A supplier's "commercial grade" description is not an approval.

Fire and egress inquiry

Identify occupancy, installation location, floor versus wall/fixture use, thickness, exposed area, substrate, adhesive and proximity to escape routes or heat sources. Ask the code professional which test, classification and edition apply to each element. E648-25a is a floor-covering critical-radiant-flux method developed for corridors and exitways and does not by itself assess fire hazard [S11]; padding on fixtures or walls may fall under different provisions (interior finish, trim, decorative materials, furnishings or foam plastics, depending on the adopted code). Never reuse one material's floor report as evidence for a fixture guard or an untested adhesive combination. Carpet and rug products have their own federal flammability tests in the U.S. [S12], which do not classify every mat or foam product.

Record report scope, specimen match, laboratory, code reference, required class, result and the authority's determination. Confirm that trims, barriers and installation staging keep the escape route. Fire approval does not imply traction or hygiene acceptance.

Cleaning validation

Before procurement, cleaning staff demonstrate the routine on a mock-up using the actual product, soil, dilution, machine/pad and drying method. Look for residue, tackiness, odour, staining, tearing, slippery film, liquid ingress, inaccessible seams and inability to dry within the operating window. Health or food-safety reviewers set any microbial or other verification; appearance alone cannot demonstrate sanitation.

Food zones

The FDA Food Code is a model code; the 2026 edition was released on 17 September 2026 [S4][S44]. Determine the locally adopted text and the requirements for the specific food preparation, storage or customer area. Do not assume an allowance or prohibition from another zone or jurisdiction. Obtain the applicable public-health determination for Canadian food premises.

Worker effort

Softer floors can increase the force needed to move carts, beds and wheelchairs [S35]. Include stocking and cleaning staff in the mock-up and record effort, not just appearance.

Material and environmental dossier

Request composition restrictions relevant to the market, emissions evidence if applicable, SDS for installation and cleaning chemicals where required, safe curing/ventilation conditions, expected degradation, repair method and disposal instructions. "Non-toxic", "eco-friendly" and "antimicrobial" need defined evidence and do not establish suitability. Do not add antimicrobial claims as a program benefit without relevant support.

Disposal and continuity

Budget for adhesive removal, substrate restoration, waste segregation and locally applicable disposal. Do not promise recycling without an accepting facility. Keep spare stock and an approved temporary control; never improvise an untested material during a supply shortage.

Document 47, Operations

Operational job aids and scenario exercises

Opening checklist — adopt and localize

Confirm designated responders and deputies; walk entrance, aisle, queue and cart-corral routes; inspect mats, seams, guards and cart straps; verify supplies and barriers; check open defects and closures; verify safe accessible and escape routes; review weather, restocking and cleaning plans; record time, findings and owner. Reopening a previously closed area needs its release, not just an opening checklist tick.

Handover checklist

Outgoing lead communicates open hazards, isolated zones, temporary routes, incomplete clean-up, repair visits, product changes and missed checks. Incoming lead acknowledges ownership and records the next required action in the handoff log. Never leave "contractor will handle it" without a named responsible role and confirmation.

Scenario A: wet entrance with curled mat

Expected response: protect customers and workers; keep safe access and egress; notify the lead; address water and the unstable mat under the approved procedure; verify before reopening; log the response and the source investigation. Wrong inference: more padding automatically fixes the hazard. Debrief: who owns saturation and replacement checks?

Scenario B: guard partly detached after cleaning

Expected response: isolate the contact area as needed, prevent access to loose parts, preserve product/batch evidence and notify facilities. Do not push it back or use an unapproved adhesive. Review cleaning compatibility and attachment; inspect matching installations. Restart only after an approved correction and verification.

Scenario C: customer reports wheelchair difficulty

Expected response: offer assistance through the site's accessible service process, record the barrier without unnecessary personal data, and escalate the route for review. Do not dismiss the complaint because a lab report passed. Stop use of a system that creates an unresolved access barrier.

Scenario D: zero injuries in month one

Expected response: report counts and exposure honestly, inspect data completeness and balancing measures, and continue the planned review. Do not display a claim that the floor prevents injuries. Ask whether reporting changed and whether exposure is enough for any inference: zero events in 100,000 entries still leaves an upper bound of about 3 per 100,000 (document 35).

Scenario E: a child-seat strap is broken

Expected response: take the cart out of service, tag it, log it in the hazard register without any customer details, and tell the cart-fleet owner. Check the rest of the corral. Wrong inference: the floor program covers cart falls.

Scenario F: someone types incident details into the workspace

Expected response: when the workspace flags a name, age, injury or claim number, edit the entry to a coded reference to the protected system. If sensitive details were already saved or exported, tell the privacy lead, remove them from the planning record and replace any exported copies.

Knowledge check and answer key

Can a warning sign alone isolate a hazard? No; use the site control procedure. Does playground testing approve a retail aisle? No. Who sets test thresholds? The competent reviewer, before testing. Who can stop use? Any trained worker starts isolation; the site manager suspends; the sponsor decides permanent withdrawal. Where do medical and claims details go? The protected organizational system.
