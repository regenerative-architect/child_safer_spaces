## National framework, provincial implementation
Use the national model codes as reference material and establish provincial or territorial adoption and local enforcement. The NRC lists the 2025 national model codes and directs readers to provincial or territorial officials to learn which codes apply [S6]. Confirm the responsible building and fire offices and any local health, worker-safety, accessibility, human-rights and occupiers' liability duties.
## Ontario illustration — not a selected pilot
Ontario's accessibility page (updated 24 March 2025) says the 2024 Building Code defines accessibility requirements for most new construction and extensive renovations, that existing buildings are not affected unless an extensive renovation is planned, and that the code works together with the Design of Public Spaces standard [S18]. Ontario's customer service guidance (updated 21 September 2026) says organizations should give public notice when accessible facilities or services are temporarily disrupted, stating the reason, how long, and any alternative, for example at the entrance or on the website [S43]. A local professional must verify the current regulation text, amendments, transition provisions and project scope before relying on either page.
Do not read the building-code treatment of existing buildings as removing separate customer-service or human-rights obligations. Conversely, do not treat every indoor floor change as falling under an outdoor public-space standard. Record the precise provisions and reasoning.

## Safety and research interfaces
CCOHS guidance treats good housekeeping as the first level of preventing slips and trips and recommends securing mats and rugs that do not lie flat [S15]; it is good practice, not the province's statute. An academic research partner should determine how TCPS 2 and institutional rules apply. TCPS 2 treats quality assurance and improvement as outside its research scope but states that pilot studies fall within its definition of research requiring REB review [S22]. Do not treat the public visibility of a store as an automatic consent exemption for an intervention study.
## Release criteria and language
Use the same evidence gates as the U.S. framework, with Canadian requirements and metric units. Provide language and access formats appropriate to the community and the applicable obligations. Do not translate U.S. legal labels or inches into a Canadian compliance statement. Record unresolved points and route them to a named local reviewer before purchasing an installed system.
