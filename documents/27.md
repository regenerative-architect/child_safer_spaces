## Substantive solution
Adopt a layered program in which each intervention answers a documented mechanism. Spend first on recurring contamination, damaged walking surfaces, obstructed routes, cart condition and avoidable fixture geometry. Test a purpose-designed protective system only after considering removal, repair, relocation and redesign. Apply the same access, fire, hygiene and durability gates to every material.
## Candidate comparison — engineering recommendations, not comparative efficacy estimates
| Candidate | Mechanism and suitable use | Main failure risks | Evidence before adoption | Position |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Leak/source repair | Stops recurrent wetting | Incomplete repair; water rerouted | Dry- and wet-weather inspection; repair acceptance | First priority where applicable |
| Entrance water management | Reduces tracked-in contamination | Saturation, curled mats, threshold conflict | Mat layout, saturation checks, change-in-level limits [S28] | Core site-specific control |
| Repair/replace damaged finish | Removes trip and surface defects | Poor bond, moisture, new transitions | Substrate investigation, approved finish, commissioning | Core remediation |
| Floor treatment/coating | Changes traction | Wears off, chemical mismatch, misleading laboratory rating | Relevant-condition testing and reapplication plan [S10] | Conditional; not a cure for leaks |
| Cart restraint use and cart maintenance | Falls from cart seats | Broken or missing straps; children unrestrained; car seats perched on carts [S32] | Fleet inspection log; observed correct restraint use [S34] | Core control where carts with child seats are used |
| Cart design and selection | Falls and tip-over | Older carts outside current design practice | Supplier evidence against the candidate cart standard F2372-15(2021) [S33] | Procurement criterion for cart replacement |
| Display stability and stocking rules | Tip-over, falling merchandise | Unanchored freestanding fixtures; heavy stock high | Anchoring per manufacturer; stocking rules; audit | Core control; owner via task T35 |
| Rounded/recessed fixtures | Removes or reduces contact geometry | New reach or clearance issues; structural modification | Manufacturer/designer approval and route review | Preferred design option |
| Commercial edge guard | May reduce local contact severity | Detachment, tearing, added projection, narrower route, cleaning access | Actual attachment, aging and cleaning evidence; protrusion and width check [S28] | Bounded candidate for a residual hazard |
| Resilient commercial flooring | May attenuate some impacts | Rolling effort, deformation, joints, fire and cleaning behaviour [S35] | Complete assembly and relevant impact evidence | Evaluate case by case |
| Engineered underlay with finish | Separates wearing layer and cushioning | Assembly mismatch, moisture trapping; FLIP found no injury benefit for one such system [S8] | Tested system with finish, underlay, adhesive and substrate | Requires system-level engineering |
| Interlocking household foam | Potential cushioning | Loose seams, uplift, loading, sanitation and fire uncertainty | Commercial evidence not normally established by domestic labelling | Not a retail specification |
| Pool noodles on fixtures | Informal padding | Weak attachment, tearing, loose parts, unknown fire behaviour | Redesign as a purpose-engineered commercial assembly | Never improvise an installation |
| Playground surfacing | Playground fall exposure | Wrong scope in retail; operational conflicts | Separate play-area design and applicable standards [S7] | Separate workstream |

## Zone examples
Grocery entrance: investigate the water source, entrance coverage and cleaning response before any cushioning. Cart corral: inspect straps and seats; post restraint reminders; remove damaged carts. Dry toy-store aisle: inspect transitions and displays; never suggest that padding makes climbing safe. Queue: relocate sharp contact points and keep turning and escape space. Restaurant: zoning and sanitation compatibility may dominate floor selection. These are design starting points, not site specifications.
