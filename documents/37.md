## Separate existing mechanisms from new proposals
**Source fact:** the IRS describes a Disabled Access Credit (Form 8826) for small businesses that earned $1 million or less or had no more than 30 full-time employees in the previous year, and a barrier-removal deduction of up to $15,000 a year for qualified expenses that remove architectural and transportation barriers for people with disabilities and older adults (page reviewed 30 June 2026) [S23]. **Unresolved:** these apply only to qualifying expenditures under their rules — not automatically to cushioning, general child-safety work or participation in this program. Obtain current-year tax advice and documentation before assuming any benefit. No tax amount is included in the calculator.
**Proposals only:** municipal, provincial or state pilot grants, shared testing vouchers, philanthropic injury-prevention funding, group procurement and insurer incentives. No available award, open application window or insurer commitment is established here. Canadian funding must be researched for the actual applicant and location rather than asserted nationwide.

## Pitch structure by funder
Business sponsor: capped learning investment, maintained legal controls and an explicit removal reserve. Public funder: shared evidence, equitable access, small-business feasibility and transparent procurement. Foundation: a defined prevention question and independent evaluation. Insurer: measurable risk controls and loss/exposure data. Manufacturer: testing/service contribution with independence and publication protections; support does not buy favourable conclusions.
## Small-business route
Offer an assessment-first option emphasizing repairs, layout, cart maintenance and operating procedures before capital flooring. Pool independent technical advice or purchasing only after each site establishes fit. Standardize paperwork and training, not assumptions about identical hazards. Budget for staff participation, accessible advisory input and removal.
## Procurement and competition
Use performance criteria and allow equivalent approaches. Declare conflicts, keep rejected-bid reasons and never use a sponsor's product as the mandatory standard. A shared testing program should define ownership of and access to results and the treatment of system variants.
