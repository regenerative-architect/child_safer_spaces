## Policy brief
Problem: everyday commercial environments contain preventable walking, cart and fixture hazards. U.S. surveillance estimated an average of about 24,000 children under 15 a year treated in emergency departments for shopping-cart injuries in 1990–2011, most with head injuries [S31]. Child-specific retail benefit from cushioning remains unestablished in this review. Proposal: support a voluntary, material-neutral demonstration program with independent evaluation and accessible design.
## Requested actions — proposals, not existing entitlements
1. Convene building, fire, accessibility, public-health, business, labour, disability and injury-prevention representatives.
2. Publish a common assessment and evidence framework without specifying one material.
3. Invite competitively selected pilots with public methods, cost reporting and adverse-effect disclosure.
4. Consider capped small-business technical assistance, testing vouchers or grants, subject to legal authority and funding.
5. Demonstrate suitable measures in public-facing procurement where permitted.
6. Review whether evidence justifies future requirements through the normal consultation and rulemaking process.

Funding should not depend on positive injury results; it should require honest reporting and safe conduct.
## Proposed evaluation criteria
Need and exposure 25%; practical maintenance capacity 20%; accessibility and community participation 20%; evaluation quality 20%; cost and replicability 15%. These are proposed weights. Require independent review and exclude unverified product marketing.
## Decision before stronger policy
Assess effect size and uncertainty, unintended harms, implementation cost, burden on small businesses, enforcement capacity, accessible alternatives and equity. Compare with enhanced existing controls. Any future performance requirement needs locally valid methods and reasonable transition arrangements; a blanket foam mandate is not supported by this package.
