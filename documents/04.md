## Evidence assessment
This is a targeted primary-source review checked on 19–20 October 2026. It is not a systematic review and no meta-analysis was attempted. Some studies were read at abstract level only; the source register states the access level for each. No quantitative estimate of child-retail injury reduction from any environmental control was found.
## What transfers to children in retail
| Evidence | Population and setting | Transfer to children in retail | Use in this program |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Contamination implicated in almost all retail slips; signs only warn [S2] | Great Britain retail, workers and public | Plausible for the mechanism; no effect size | Core control: source removal, barriers, cleaning in sections |
| Shopping-cart injuries: about 24,000 U.S. children under 15 a year in emergency departments, 1990–2011; head injuries 78% [S31] | U.S. national surveillance (abstract level) | Direct population and setting; no store denominators | Route cart controls (T35); measure restraint use, not just floors |
| Children under five, 2003–2008: falls from carts 82%, tip-overs 4%, head/face 84%, hospital admission 3% [S32] | CPSC analysis of national surveillance | Direct; surveillance estimates with sampling error | Prioritize cart falls over floor cushioning |
| Store greeters plus a coupon raised correct restraint use from 15% to 49%; half still unrestrained [S34] | Seven U.S. stores, 2006 (secondary news release) | Direct setting; process outcome only, injuries not measured | Candidate operational control to evaluate |
| Compliant flooring trial: no effect on serious fall injuries (rate ratio 1.04, 95% CI 0.45–2.39); falls 1.21 (0.87–1.68); harms not assessed [S8] | Older adults, one long-term care site | Low; different users, product and setting | Do not claim cushioning efficacy |
| Compliant flooring cuts laboratory impact forces but may raise fall risk and the force needed to push carts, beds and wheelchairs [S35] | Older adults and health-care workers (scoping review) | Mechanisms transfer: wheeled loads and balance | Treat rolling effort and stability as pass/fail |
| Multi-component worker program followed by 58% fewer slip, trip and fall claims; no comparison group reported [S36] | Hospital employees, conference abstract | Partial: housekeeping parts may transfer; footwear does not | Supports operational controls; not a customer estimate |
| Playground surfacing method addresses head-injury risk in a playground use zone; laboratory results do not account for aging or maintenance [S7] | Playground equipment | None for retail aisles | Separate play-area workstream only |
## Hazard map
| Hazard | Mechanism | Program decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wet or contaminated floor | Slip | Source control, cleaning, physical isolation, accessible alternative (document 12) |
| Broken, shifting or curling surface; mat edges | Trip | Repair; fixed or recessed matting; changes in level within limits [S28] |
| Soft, cushioned or loose surface | Trip, instability, rolling effort | Access and wheeled-load review is pass/fail [S28][S35] |
| Fixture corners and edges in the child head-height band | Collision | Remove, round, recess or relocate first; guards only through G3 |
| Cart seats and restraints | Fall from height | Restraint use, cart maintenance, design per candidate standard (document 27) |
| Displays, stacked stock, freestanding fixtures | Tip-over, falling objects | Stability, anchoring and stocking rules; owner assigned (T35) |
| Doors, escalators, stairs, play areas | Entrapment, falls | Specialist owner and applicable rules |
## Site survey
Map each hazard by zone, mechanism, exposure, existing control, suspected injury route, photographs without people, and immediate action. Survey fixture edges from the floor up to at least the tallest planned child height: for children aged two to five, WHO growth standards put the 97th-percentile height at about 92–93 cm at age two and about 118–119 cm at age five [S37], so a 0–120 cm band is a reasonable **program default** for that age group. Raise the band for older children. Observe restocking and cleaning as well as trading, and review small-wheel and cane-tip interactions.
## Risk triage — program method
Use severity 1–5 and likelihood 1–5 only to sort work. Describe each rating and its uncertainty; multiplying them is not a legal safety determination. Potentially serious hazards, blocked escape or accessible routes, loose parts and unstable walking surfaces are isolated immediately regardless of score. Other work gets an owner and due date; overdue actions escalate to the site manager.
## Evidence gaps to commission
Child-retail incidence by mechanism with store denominators; effectiveness of edge guards and fixture redesign; loaded-cart and mobility performance of cushioned systems; aged, wet and cleaned performance; guard durability; life-cycle costs; and comparative injury outcomes. Assign these questions (document 32) before claiming effectiveness.
