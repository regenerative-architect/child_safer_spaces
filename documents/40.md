## Audit scope
Audit whether the actual installed system, people and procedures match the approved program. Sampling cannot prove the absence of all hazards. Audit high-risk zones and adverse findings deliberately; do not sample only the easiest sites. Proposed cadence: commissioning, first month, formal pilot review, and after material changes or serious concerns, plus risk-based independent reviews.
## Audit checklist
| Area | Evidence to inspect | Failure requiring action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Configuration | As-built, lot IDs, approved substitutions | Unreviewed material/adhesive change |
| Routes | Actual layout during busy/restocking periods | Obstruction, narrowed route or new access/escape barrier |
| Carts | Strap and seat condition, removal of defective carts | Defective cart in service |
| Maintenance | Logs, observed work, chemical compatibility | Missing inspections, incompatible treatment |
| Product condition | Seams, movement, guards, ingress | Detachment, instability, contamination |
| Training | Demonstration and shift coverage | Staff cannot perform the critical response |
| Data | Event/exposure windows, duplicates, missingness | Invalid rates or understated missing data |
| Privacy | Workspace registers and exports | Personal or sensitive details stored in planning records |
| Claims | Notices, presentations and partner references | Unsupported efficacy or certification claim |
| Governance | Open actions and signed releases | Gate treated as complete without evidence |
## Finding classification — program definitions
Critical: immediate serious safety, access or egress concern, or an unaccepted system creating plausible harm; isolate and escalate. Major: required process or evidence absent, or systematic failure; prompt containment and sponsor review. Minor: bounded documentation or process defect without a known critical condition; correct by the assigned date. Review facts rather than relying on labels to delay protection.

## Effectiveness check
Verify the cause is addressed, not merely that a task was marked done. Example: after replacing a curling mat, check whether saturation, cart movement or fit caused recurrence. Sample later operating periods and cleaning cycles. Reopen the finding if the defect returns. Link recurring product issues across sites and batches.
