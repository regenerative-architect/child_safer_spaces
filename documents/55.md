## How to use this glossary
Plain-language definitions of the words used in this package. The workspace has a searchable version (Glossary). This document is generated from source/content.json; edit the glossary there, or keep a local draft here.

| Accessible route | A path that people using wheelchairs, walkers, canes or strollers can actually use. | Width, slope, surface, level changes and protruding objects are set by the accessibility rules that apply to the site. | Document 30 |
| Adverse effect | A harm caused by the change itself, such as a new trip edge or harder cart pushing. | Pilots must look for and report adverse effects as well as benefits. | Document 15 |
| Aggregate data | Counts and rates about groups, never about named people. | This workspace only holds aggregate, non-identifying planning information. | Document 16 |

| Child survey band | The zone from the floor to 120 cm where the kit looks for edges and corners at young children's height. | Set from WHO growth standards [S37]: tall five-year-olds are about 118–119 cm (97th percentile). Raise it for older children. | Document 28 |
| Coded reference | A short code that points to a record kept in a protected system, instead of copying the details here. | For example INC-REF-0042 rather than a child's name or injury. | Document 18 |
| Comparison group | Similar sites or periods without the change, used to judge whether the change made a difference. | Without one, a drop in incidents may be chance or a trend. | Document 35 |

| Design effect | How much bigger a study must be because sites, not single visits, are compared. | A design effect of 2 doubles the number of events needed. | Document 35 |
| DCOF | Dynamic coefficient of friction: a measure of how slippery a hard floor is while something slides on it. | Results depend on the method, the device and the conditions; a number alone is not a safety verdict. | Document 29 |
| Disability-adjusted life year | One year of healthy life lost to illness, disability or early death. | WHO's falls fact sheet uses it to describe the burden of falls [S45]. | Document 50 |

| Hazard | Something that could hurt someone, such as a wet patch, a loose mat or a sharp shelf corner. | The hazard register records the hazard, who it affects and what was done. | Workspace: Planning registers |
| IndexedDB | A database built into your browser, where this workspace keeps your planning work on your own device. | If the browser does not allow it, the workspace falls back to simpler browser storage, then to memory only. | Workspace: Your data |
| Jurisdiction | The place whose laws apply: a country, state, province or city. | Most rules in this kit are U.S. or Canadian worked examples; other places need their own review. | Workspace: Adapt to your country |
| Material-neutral | Not favouring any product or material; the kit says what a solution must achieve, not what to buy. | Products are compared against written performance criteria (document 08). | Document 08 |
| Net present value | Future costs and savings added up in today's money. | A discount rate turns later amounts into present amounts; the calculator shows ranges, not a forecast. | Workspace: Cost scenarios |
| Performance-based | Describing what has to be achieved, and how it will be checked, instead of naming a product. | Acceptance criteria are written before buying anything (gate G3). | Document 08 |
| Pilot | A small, time-limited trial at a few sites, with a plan to stop or reverse it if needed. | A pilot tests whether a change can be run well; it usually cannot prove fewer injuries. | Document 10 |

| Protruding object | Something sticking out into a walking route, such as a shelf edge or sign, that a person may not see. | Accessibility rules limit how far objects may protrude at certain heights. | Document 24 |
| Quarantine (of saved data) | When saved work cannot be read, the workspace keeps an untouched copy and stops saving over it. | You can download the copy, then start fresh or import a backup. | Workspace: Your data |
| Rate ratio | One rate divided by another; 0.8 means 20% lower. | Used in evaluation planning to describe the change you want to detect. | Workspace: Evaluation planning |
| Removal reserve | Money set aside in advance to take a change out again if it causes problems. | Required before installation so that stopping is always affordable. | Document 17 |
| Service worker | A small browser helper that keeps a copy of the workspace files so a hosted copy opens without a connection. | It is used only when the workspace is served from a secure web address; it never stores your planning data. | Workspace: Your data |
| Slip resistance | How well a floor stops feet from sliding. | It changes with water, cleaning products, wear and footwear, so it is checked in use, not only in a lab. | Document 12 |
| Statistical power | The chance that a study detects a real effect of the size you care about. | Small pilots usually have low power for injuries, which is why the kit sets realistic aims. | Document 35 |
| Surveillance (injury) | Systematic collection of injury counts, for example from hospital emergency departments. | It counts injuries; it does not watch people. Store CCTV is a separate, sensitive matter. | Document 15 |
| Tribometer | A device that measures how slippery a floor is. | Different devices give different numbers; the method must be validated and agreed in advance. | Document 29 |
| Zero events | No injuries recorded during a period. | Zero events are not proof of safety: small pilots often see none either way. | Document 35 |
