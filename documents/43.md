## Research versus operations
Routine hazard correction proceeds through existing safety processes. A planned intervention study, structured participant testing or secondary data research needs an applicable ethics and privacy determination before that activity begins. U.S. federal regulations define research as a systematic investigation, including development, testing and evaluation, designed to develop or contribute to generalizable knowledge [S41]. OHRP explains that most quality-improvement work is not research under those regulations but that intent to publish alone does not decide the question [S21]. Canada's TCPS 2 treats quality assurance and improvement and program evaluation as outside its research scope, but states that pilot studies fall within its definition of research requiring REB review [S22]. None of these sources grants an exemption for this project.

## Participant protection
Use adults for initial usability discovery. Separate consent from access to ordinary services or employment expectations. Explain withdrawal, recording and compensation. Avoid collecting personal injury stories in public feedback. Research involving children needs separate expertise, parental permission/assent arrangements and safeguarding review as applicable; this pack does not authorize it.

## Minimum analytical dataset
Prefer site-period aggregate counts, exposure, intervention dates and operational measures. If coded event-level data is necessary, justify each field and keep keys separate; coded data can remain identifiable in context. Keep time and location granularity only as needed and assess linkage risks. Removing names is not sufficient anonymization.
## Release gate
The privacy officer and evaluation lead confirm that analysis and publication match the approved purpose, disclosures and data controls. Keep negative results; protect identities. An organizational confidentiality review must not become a hidden way to suppress unfavourable findings.
