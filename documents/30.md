**Workflow:** W08 and W10 in document 49. Needs a qualified accessibility reviewer.
## Purpose
Assess whether the intervention keeps safe and dignified movement for everyone. Legal access review and practical usability review are complementary; neither a satisfied volunteer nor a code checklist alone establishes all-user suitability.

## U.S. dimensions that padding and guards commonly affect
**Source fact** (2010 ADA Standards [S28]; scoping and stricter rules decided locally):
| Requirement | Value | Why it matters here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accessible route clear width | 36 in (915 mm) minimum; 32 in (815 mm) allowed for up to 24 in (610 mm) | Guards on both sides of an aisle can take the width below the minimum |
| Protruding objects | Leading edges 27–80 in (685–2030 mm) above the floor may protrude 4 in (100 mm) maximum; protrusions may not reduce required clear width | Padding adds projection that a cane may not detect |
| Headroom | 80 in (2030 mm) minimum | Overhead padding or signs |
| Changes in level | ≤ 1/4 in vertical; ≤ 1/2 in bevelled no steeper than 1:2; larger must be ramped | Mat and underlay edges |
| Carpet | Securely attached; firm cushion, pad or backing, or none; pile ≤ 1/2 in; exposed edges trimmed | Soft or cushioned coverings on routes |
In Canada, use the adopted provincial code and, where it applies, CSA/ASC B651:23 [S27]; do not convert U.S. dimensions into a Canadian compliance statement.
## Controlled mock-up tasks
First inspect the configuration without users. Then, if accepted for testing, trained consenting adults — including disabled advisors using their own devices, where they agree — assess approach, entry, straight travel, turning, stopping, fixture reach and exit. Include representative manual and powered wheelchairs, walkers, canes, strollers and loaded carts. A few devices do not represent every user. No induced slips or falls, no child impact trials and no excessive exertion.

## Review conditions
Predetermine tolerances with the relevant professional rather than inventing a universal acceptable increase in push force. Compliant flooring can raise the force needed to push carts, beds and wheelchairs [S35]. Inability to navigate, wheel or cane trapping, unexpected instability, pain or fatigue, or a new barrier suspends that test for review. A smooth alternate route must not become an inferior, obstructed or segregating service route.
## Fixture and visual review
Check added guard thickness, circulation-path projections, cane detection [S24], visual contrast, glare and patterns that could be misread as steps or holes. Review the real fixture layout; cushioning can worsen projection hazards.
## During works and closures
Keep accessible features in operable condition; interruptions must be isolated or temporary [S29]. Offer assistance such as retrieving goods when an aisle is closed [S14], and post notice of temporary disruptions where required (in Ontario, with reason, duration and alternatives [S43]).
