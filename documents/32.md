## Status of the evidence
This pack uses a targeted review of official guidance, regulations, standards scopes, surveillance reports and a small number of primary studies. It does not establish a pooled child-retail injury reduction, a preferred commercial product or a universal benefit-cost ratio. A plausible biomechanical effect and a retail clinical benefit are different claims.
## Evidence categories used
VERIFIED SOURCE FACT: a narrow statement supported by the cited source as read (access level recorded). PROGRAM RECOMMENDATION: our proposed operating rule or engineering approach. ILLUSTRATION: invented inputs used to explain a method. UNRESOLVED: information the site must obtain. These labels are not formal GRADE ratings. Every source keeps its population, setting, product and jurisdiction limits.

## Search record for this review
Targeted searches and retrievals on 19–20 October 2026 covered regulator and standards pages, U.S. and Canadian surveillance programs, shopping-cart injury studies, compliant-flooring trials and reviews, and workplace slip-trip-fall programs. One literature database was not reachable (rate-limited), so a press release was used for one study and labelled as secondary. This is a record of a targeted review, not a systematic search.
## Reproducible review to commission
Question: in public-facing retail environments involving children, which environmental or operational controls reduce same-level falls, cart falls, collisions or injury severity without increasing access or worker burdens? Define interventions, comparators, age groups, outcomes and eligible designs before searching. Search health and engineering databases and trial registries plus regulator sources; log the exact query, platform, date and coverage. Include adverse and null findings; search references and manufacturer evidence with conflict flags. Two reviewers independently screen and extract; document exclusions and resolve disagreements.
Extract population, setting, system, comparator, denominators, duration, outcome definitions, effect and interval, confounding, attrition, reporting changes and funding. Separate child-retail evidence from adult-care and laboratory evidence. Assess design-appropriate bias; do not combine incompatible outcomes. Do not call the review complete until the search and screening records exist.
## Burden-data research
NEISS can inform product-associated emergency-department injury patterns [S19]; CHIRPP records what the injured person was doing and where, from 20 mostly paediatric hospitals, with coverage limits [S20]. Neither supplies store visits or proves a product caused an injury. Predefine narrative coding, duplicate handling and ambiguous-setting rules; obtain a methods review before weighted estimates. No national retail-child total is invented in this pack.
