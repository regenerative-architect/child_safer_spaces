## Recruitment and consent introduction
We are seeking voluntary feedback from adult caregivers, disabled customers, older customers and staff about walking routes, carts and proposed improvements. This is feedback, not a safety test. You may skip questions or stop at any time. Please do not give a child's name or medical details; personal incidents go through the site's protected reporting channel. We explain any recording, retention and compensation before you agree.
## Caregiver/customer questions
1. Which areas are hardest to get through, and when?
2. What makes you feel confident that hazards are handled?
3. How do you usually seat or carry young children while shopping, and what gets in the way of using the cart strap?
4. Looking at [neutral description of proposed change], what seems useful and what concerns you?
5. Would it affect your ability to use a mobility aid, stroller or cart? How?
6. How would you prefer to report a concern?
7. What evidence would you need before believing the change improves safety?
8. Is anything missing from this proposal?
## Staff/manager questions
What recurring hazards are hardest to control? Which shift conditions matter? Can the proposed inspection and cleaning work fit available staffing? Which carts have broken or missing straps? What tools or training are missing? What would prompt you to stop use? What maintenance cost or disruption would make this impractical?
## Insurer/government questions
What evidence is necessary for your decision? Which approvals or constraints are often missed? Which parts could be standardized? What incentives are within your authority, and what would need new policy or funding?
## Proposed discovery sample and analysis
Start with roughly 5–8 adults in each relevant group as qualitative discovery, with disability representation and varied visit times. This is a convenience sample, not representative polling. Report group counts, recruitment, non-response and themes, including objections. Do not present preference percentages as population estimates. Revise messages and design, then test with new participants.
