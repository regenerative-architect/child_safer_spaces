## Why a pathway
Good ideas cause harm when they spread faster than the evidence. This pathway describes how Child-Safer Spaces can move from one site to an organization, a city, a country and international exchange, and what must be true before each step. **Program rule:** move to the next stage only when the evidence for the current one exists, never on enthusiasm alone.

## The six stages
| Stage | What happens | Who decides | Needed before moving on | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Explore | Someone reads the brief (document 50) and tries the workspace | Nobody; no commitment | A named sponsor willing to run a pilot | Mistaking the kit for an approval |
| 2. Site pilot | One to a few sites work through gates G0 to G5 | The site sponsor, in writing | A completed pilot review (G5) with costs, adverse effects and limits | Changes that reduce access or burden workers |
| 3. Organization | An organization repeats the pilot at more of its own sites | The executive sponsor (G6) | Results from several sites, including null or negative findings | Copying a fix to sites with different conditions |
| 4. City or region | Officials convene several organizations and offer local guidance | The relevant authorities, through their usual processes | Consultation with disabled people's organizations, workers, families and small businesses | Turning a voluntary pilot into a de facto requirement |
| 5. Country | A national agency or association publishes adapted guidance (document 53) | National authorities and associations | Locally valid methods; reviewed translations; an independent evaluation | Exporting U.S. or Canadian rules as if they applied |
| 6. International exchange | Countries share adaptations, methods and results | Each participant for its own work | A chosen licence for sharing, and honest reporting of results | Claims of proven benefit before evaluations exist |

## Ways to help, by who you are
| If you are | You can help by | Start with | Please avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| An elected official or policy adviser | Asking for a voluntary pilot with honest reporting; protecting time for consultation | Document 50 | Announcing benefits before evaluation |
| A public-health or injury-prevention agency | Sharing aggregate injury data and evaluation expertise | Documents 15, 35 and 53 | Requesting identifiable records |
| A building, fire or accessibility official | Answering "which rules apply here?" for pilot sites | Documents 06 and 07 | Approving by silence or informal remarks |
| A workplace-safety regulator or inspector | Reviewing cleaning, cart handling and ergonomics | Document 30 | Treating worker burden as out of scope |
| A retail, property or business association | Recruiting a mix of large and small pilot sites | Documents 02 and 46 | Presenting the kit as an industry standard |
| An insurer or risk adviser | Reviewing pilots and sharing loss-prevention experience | Document 19 | Implying premium discounts that do not exist |
| A disabled people's organization | Reviewing proposals and testing mock-ups | Documents 30 and 24 | Being consulted after decisions are made; ask to be involved early |
| A worker representative or union | Reviewing procedures, training and staffing | Documents 12, 13 and 30 | Accepting new duties without time or tools |
| A parents', carers' or child-safety organization | Reviewing public wording and sharing families' experience | Documents 23 and 24 | Blame-based messages about carers |
| A researcher or university | Designing an independent evaluation with honest power limits | Documents 15, 32, 35 and 43 | Evaluating a pilot you have a stake in without disclosure |
| A standards body | Considering whether a performance method is needed | Documents 08 and 29 | Copying program defaults as thresholds |
| A translator or interpreter | Reviewing the draft French and Spanish briefs, or translating others | Documents 51, 52 and 53 | Publishing unreviewed translations as official |
| A developer or IT team | Checking the workspace in your browsers and hosting it safely | The maintainer guide (source/README.md) | Adding tracking, accounts or network calls |
| A funder or foundation | Funding evaluation, accessibility participation and removal reserves | Documents 20 and 37 | Making funding depend on positive results |

## Spreading responsibly
- **Program rule:** participation is voluntary. Nobody should be pressured to join, to stay or to report favourably.
- **Program rule:** no endorsement, partnership or approval exists unless it is in writing from the organization concerned.
- **Program rule:** no product is endorsed. Suppliers may meet published performance criteria; they may not claim the program recommends them.
- **Program rule:** publish adverse effects and null results as readily as good news.
- **Program rule:** local ownership. Each place adapts the kit to its own rules and people, and records its decisions.
- Before anyone republishes or translates the package, the owner needs to choose a licence. Until then, ask the owner before copying it beyond reading and internal evaluation.

## Outreach
Document 42 contains ready-to-adapt messages and an outreach sequence. For international outreach, translate and review a message before sending it, name the program as a draft planning kit, attach the two-page brief, and never claim that another organization supports the program unless it has said so in writing.

## What progress looks like
Honest signs of progress, none of which is a claim about injuries:
- sites with a complete gate record and a removal reserve;
- consultations completed before changes, with their responses recorded;
- hazards found, fixed and checked again;
- costs reported, including cleaning and worker time;
- evaluations completed and published, whatever the result;
- translations reviewed and approved locally.
