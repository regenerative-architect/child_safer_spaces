## Who this is for
Officials, agencies, associations and organizations that want to use Child-Safer Spaces outside the United States and Canada, or in a large organization with its own rules. The rest of the package uses U.S. and Canadian laws and standards as worked examples. This kit shows how to replace them with your own without losing the program's safeguards.

## What stays the same everywhere
- **Program rule:** performance-based and material-neutral. Write what a fix must achieve and how it will be checked; never name a product as the answer.
- **Program rule:** no safety change may reduce access for disabled visitors or make work harder or riskier for workers.
- **Program rule:** non-sensitive planning only. No children's identities, injury or health details, footage, witness details or claim numbers in this workspace.
- **Program rule:** decisions run through gates G0 to G6, and a checkbox is never a signature.
- **Program rule:** honest evaluation. Report costs, adverse effects and null results; never claim fewer injuries without an evaluation that can show it.

## What changes
Laws and their editions, standards and test methods, the authorities who decide, injury data sources, languages and sign conventions, consultation bodies, funding routes and procurement rules.

## Step 1. Map the rules that apply
For each area, find the rule, its edition and the authority that applies it. Record each one as a row in the jurisdiction register (type LAW, CONTRACT, PROGRAM or PROPOSAL) with the edition and a recheck date.

| Area | What to find | Usually decided by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Building and accessibility | Adopted building code, accessibility rules, protruding-object and route rules, local amendments | Building authority; accessibility office |
| Fire | Rules for floor coverings and fixture materials, exit routes | Fire authority |
| Workplace safety | Duties for walking surfaces, cleaning, manual handling and training | Labour inspectorate or workplace-safety regulator |
| Consumer and product safety | Rules for carts, fixtures and products children use or touch | Consumer-safety or market-surveillance authority |
| Food hygiene (where food is sold) | Floor and cleaning requirements in food areas | Food or health inspectorate |
| Privacy and data protection | Rules for incident records, footage and research data | Data-protection authority |
| Research ethics | When an evaluation counts as research and needs review | Research ethics committee |
| Public procurement (for public buildings) | How works and products may be bought | Procurement office |

If you cannot find a rule, record it as UNDETERMINED with the question you asked and who you asked. Silence from an authority is never approval.

## Step 2. Use international references as context
These references help you explain the program and find local equivalents. None of them approves a site change.

| Reference | How it helps | What it does not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WHO falls fact sheet [S45] and World report on child injury prevention [S46] [S47] | Shows why child falls matter and which prevention approaches exist | Gives no retail data; the report notes weak trial evidence for environmental changes in homes [S46] |
| WHO injury surveillance guidelines [S55] | Practical advice for setting up injury data collection, including with few resources | Does not set privacy rules or retail exposure measures |
| UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, articles 4 and 9 [S48] | Access to facilities open to the public, and consultation with disabled people, including children | Binds States Parties, not businesses directly; ratification varies |
| SDG target 11.7 [S49] | Policy language on safe, inclusive and accessible spaces for children and disabled people | Its indicator measures urban open space, not shops |
| ILO fundamental principle on a safe and healthy working environment [S54] | Places worker safety at the centre of site changes | National law and ratification still decide duties |
| ISO 21542 [S50] and ISO/IEC Guide 71 [S52] | International references for accessible buildings and accessibility in standards | Not adopted everywhere; texts not audited here |
| ISO/IEC Guide 50 [S51] | Child-safety considerations for people who write product standards | Sets no thresholds for sites |
| ISO 7010 [S53] | Registered safety signs that can replace text-only warnings | A sign warns; it does not control a hazard |
| WCAG 2.2 [S56] | If you publish the workspace on an agency website, a reference for checking accessibility | Not a conformance statement for this package |

## Step 3. Translate test methods, not just words
The package names U.S. and Canadian test methods (for example ASTM, ANSI, CSA and ULC methods). Your country may use national or international equivalents. A qualified person must confirm each equivalence in writing in the standards register. Never convert a threshold by changing units alone, and never adopt a threshold the package marks as unresolved.

## Step 4. Find local injury information
Ask your health ministry, hospitals or national injury surveillance programme which aggregate data exist for falls and impacts in shops and public buildings. The WHO guidelines [S55] describe how surveillance systems are built. Request counts and rates only. Store any approved data-sharing agreement in your protected system and record only its reference here.

## Step 5. Consult before you adapt
Invite, early and with enough time to respond:
- disabled people's organizations, including those representing disabled children [S48];
- worker representatives and workplace-safety committees (a safe and healthy working environment is an ILO fundamental principle [S54]);
- parents' and carers' organizations and child-safety groups;
- businesses of different sizes, landlords and cleaning contractors;
- building, fire, health and workplace authorities.
Record each consultation in the consultation register: the question, the response reference, any binding condition and what is still unresolved. Use workflow W06 for the steps.

## Step 6. Run a pre-mortem
Imagine it is two years later and the program has caused harm. Ask what happened, then add a safeguard and an owner for each answer. Record the results in the decisions register.

| What could go wrong | Example | Safeguard to consider |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Access gets worse | A soft mat creates an edge that catches wheelchair castors | Accessibility review before any change; disabled people test mock-ups (G3) |
| Workers carry the cost | Extra cleaning or harder cart pushing without more time or tools | Worker review; ergonomic checks; staffing in the budget |
| Data creep | Staff start recording children's names or footage "to be safe" | The personal-data check; coded references; training |
| Blame shifts to families | Signs or staff imply carers are at fault | Public wording review (document 23); no blame language |
| False confidence | A pilot with zero events is announced as proof of safety | Claims register; evaluation planning (document 35) |
| Small businesses are left out | Requirements copied from large chains | Proportionate options; technical help; no mandate without consultation |
| Product capture | A supplier markets the program as an endorsement | No endorsements; material-neutral specifications (document 08) |

## Step 7. Translate and review the materials
1. Decide which documents your users need first. A good order is: this brief (document 50), the public explanation kit (document 23), daily procedures (document 12) and the feedback forms (document 24).
2. Use qualified translators and a native-speaker review by someone who knows the field. Keep plain language.
3. In the workspace, open each document, edit a local draft in the new language, and use the comparison to check nothing was lost. Export the working handbook to share the draft for review.
4. Replace text-only warnings with the safety signs your rules require, for example registered signs from ISO 7010 where adopted [S53].
5. Record which language versions are approved, by whom, as a decision. Draft translations included in this package (documents 51 and 52) have not yet been reviewed.

## Step 8. Record decisions and continue
Record the rules, standards, consultations and translation decisions in the registers, then complete gate G2 for each pilot site. Use document 54 for the route from one site to many.

## What you can skip for now
Site checklists, commissioning forms and cost scenarios can wait until the rules that apply have been recorded.
