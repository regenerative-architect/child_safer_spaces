## Value propositions to validate
| Audience | Proposed appeal | Proof needed | First ask |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Parents/caregivers | Fewer avoidable hazards during everyday visits, including carts | Visible fixes, credible reporting, honest limits | Review a walk-through and feedback form |
| Disabled visitors/older adults | Safer routes without extra mobility effort | User involvement, accessible transitions and routes | Paid adult accessibility advisory session |
| Store staff | Clear response duties and fewer repeat hazards | Workable time, training and repair support | Join a shift walkthrough and usability trial |
| Business leadership | Manageable risk and predictable whole-life costs | Budget, operating feasibility, honest outcome data | Fund a bounded assessment, then decide |
| Insurer/self-insured risk team | Auditable prevention and reliable data | Exposure-adjusted trends, maintenance and product evidence | Agree evidence criteria in writing |
| Government/public health | Prevention with equitable access | Independent results and feasible enforcement | Support evaluation and coordination |
| Suppliers/landlords | Clear specifications and responsibility | Traceable acceptance and maintenance terms | Provide dossier and written permissions |
## Adoption sequence
Recruit one sponsor and a small set of suitable sites; engage disability representatives and staff before selecting products. Agree evidence questions with the insurer and authorities, then obtain competing supplier responses. Publish a factual notice before any physical pilot. Review operational findings at 30, 60 and 90 days. Expand only after Gate G6; replication should include different exposure levels and store sizes.
## Selection rubric
Score each site 0–2 for documented hazard burden, data quality, management capacity, representative traffic, feasible comparison, and ability to isolate and reverse changes. These are program screening scores, not risk certifications. Exclude sites that cannot keep a safe accessible route or fund removal. Include smaller businesses when maintenance capacity can be supported.
## Prevent adoption friction
Offer standard documents, shared testing, training time and procurement options. Keep public claims specific. Do not promise lower premiums, tax credits, grants or a universal return on investment. A large retailer is an example, not an assumed partner.
